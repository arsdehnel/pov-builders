---
title: Mental Health
order: 7
description: Whether mental health is treated with parity alongside physical health in terms of coverage, access, and clinical investment — and the persistent gaps between mental health need and available care.
---

The Mental Health Parity and Addiction Equity Act requires that insurance coverage for mental health and substance use be no more restrictive than for physical health. In practice, the gap remains substantial: out-of-network rates for mental health providers are far higher than for physical health specialists, provider shortages are severe, and prior authorization requirements create barriers that are rarely imposed on comparably evidenced physical treatments. The legal requirement exists; meaningful parity does not.

The demand for mental health services has outpaced supply in ways that crisis responses haven't resolved. For healthcare organizations, decisions about how to staff, fund, and integrate mental health care reflect a genuine values question: whether behavioral health is treated as a core service or a referral destination, whether it's integrated into primary care or siloed in a separate department, and whether people with serious mental illness or substance use disorders receive care proportional to their clinical complexity.
