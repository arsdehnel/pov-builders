---
title: Access and Equity
order: 1
description: Whether all people can get the care they need regardless of income, location, insurance status, race, or language — and the gap between who healthcare systems claim to serve and who actually receives effective care.
---

The United States spends more on healthcare per capita than any other wealthy country and achieves worse outcomes on most population-level measures. Much of that gap is driven by access failures: uninsured and underinsured people delaying care until it's urgent and expensive, geographic deserts where primary care is unavailable, and systemic barriers that make navigating the healthcare system itself a full-time job for people without education, time, or English fluency.

Access and equity are related but not identical. Expanding access to a system that delivers unequal care by race, income, and language is necessary but not sufficient. Disparities in outcomes — maternal mortality, diabetes management, cancer screening rates — persist even when controlling for access, reflecting how care is delivered once patients are in the door. Equity asks not just who can get care, but whether care is as good for everyone who receives it.
