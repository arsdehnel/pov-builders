---
title: End-of-Life Care
order: 11
description: How healthcare systems approach the final stages of life — advance directives, palliative care, overtreatment, and whether care at end of life aligns with patients' values or institutional defaults.
---

A substantial portion of US healthcare spending occurs in the final months of life, much of it on aggressive interventions that research consistently shows most patients, if asked, would not want. The mismatch between what patients say they want — comfort, time with family, dignity — and what they receive — hospitalizations, procedures, intensive care — reflects a system built around treatment capacity and fee-for-service incentives rather than patient goals.

Palliative care, hospice, and advance care planning are the clinical frameworks for aligning end-of-life care with patient values. All three are underused. Palliative care improves quality of life and often extends it; it is too often introduced only in the final days. Advance directives document patient wishes; they're too often unavailable or ignored at the moment they're needed. How healthcare organizations build, staff, and prioritize end-of-life care reflects whether patient autonomy is a stated value or a practiced one.
