---
title: Environment and Health
order: 10
description: The environmental determinants of health outcomes — air quality, water safety, climate change, and toxic exposure — and whether healthcare systems engage with the conditions that drive the illness they treat.
---

Environmental conditions are among the strongest determinants of health outcomes. Air pollution drives respiratory disease and cardiovascular events. Lead exposure causes irreversible cognitive harm. Heat, flooding, and wildfire smoke — accelerated by climate change — create new and intensifying health burdens that fall hardest on communities with the least capacity to adapt. Healthcare systems treat the results of these exposures; they rarely address their causes.

Climate change is both a healthcare operations challenge and a public health emergency. Healthcare facilities are major energy consumers and generate significant waste — the healthcare sector accounts for roughly 8% of US greenhouse gas emissions. Organizations that commit to environmental sustainability as part of their mission face a genuine alignment question: they're being asked to reduce their own footprint while their communities face growing climate-driven health needs, with inadequate resources to address either.
