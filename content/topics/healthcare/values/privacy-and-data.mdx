---
title: Privacy and Data
order: 3
description: How health information is collected, stored, shared, and used — and the balance between data-driven care improvement and patients' control over sensitive personal information.
---

Health data is among the most sensitive personal information people generate. Electronic health records, insurance claims, pharmacy records, and consumer health apps create a detailed longitudinal picture of a person's body, behavior, and mental state. That data has significant value for clinical improvement, research, and care coordination — and for insurance underwriting, employers, and data brokers, in ways patients rarely understand or consent to.

HIPAA establishes minimum protections, but its coverage has significant gaps, particularly for consumer health apps and data that flows outside the traditional healthcare system. Healthcare organizations that handle sensitive data — mental health, reproductive health, substance use — carry a particular responsibility. How they design access controls, respond to breaches, and communicate with patients about data use reflects their values in practice, not just on paper.
