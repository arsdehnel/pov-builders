---
title: Workforce and Burnout
order: 9
description: The working conditions of healthcare workers and the relationship between staff wellbeing, turnover, and the quality of care patients receive.
---

Healthcare worker burnout has reached crisis levels in the United States. Moral injury — the distress that comes from being unable to deliver the care you believe patients deserve — combines with documentation burden, staffing shortages, and pandemic-era trauma to produce attrition rates that are reshaping the workforce. Nurses are leaving hospital settings for agency work or leaving healthcare entirely; primary care physicians are retiring early or reducing hours.

The connection between workforce conditions and patient care isn't abstract. Understaffed units produce errors. High turnover interrupts care continuity. Clinicians who are stretched too thin make different decisions than those with adequate time per patient. Healthcare organizations that treat workforce investment as overhead rather than a quality strategy produce worse care — eventually visibly, often before they acknowledge it.
