---
title: Cost and Affordability
order: 2
description: The financial burden of healthcare on patients, employers, and public systems — whether cost functions as a barrier to necessary care or a reasonable constraint on resource allocation.
---

Healthcare costs in the US are both higher and less transparent than in peer countries. Administrative complexity, opaque pricing, and the absence of effective price negotiation have produced a system where the cost of a procedure can vary by an order of magnitude depending on where you receive it, who insures you, and whether you know to ask. High-deductible plans have shifted more of that cost directly onto patients, with predictable effects: people skip necessary care because they can't afford the out-of-pocket share.

For healthcare organizations, cost pressure is a constant tension. Efficiency improvements that reduce cost can undermine quality if they cut care coordination, reduce clinician time per patient, or shift burden to patients and families. How organizations balance cost discipline with care quality and access — and whose costs and benefits enter the calculation — is a defining choice that shows up in every operational and investment decision.
