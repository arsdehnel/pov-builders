---
title: Prevention and Population Health
order: 6
description: Prioritizing interventions that keep people healthy rather than treating them when they're sick — and the tension between individual clinical care and the upstream conditions that drive population-level outcomes.
---

The majority of health outcomes are determined by factors outside the clinical encounter: housing stability, food access, air and water quality, income, early childhood environment. The healthcare system is designed to treat the results of those conditions, not to change them. Preventive care — vaccines, screenings, chronic disease management — reduces downstream costs and suffering, but it operates within the same downstream logic.

Population health asks what it would take to actually improve health at the community level. For healthcare organizations, that means thinking about where their patients live, work, and eat; engaging with the social services and community organizations that address root causes; and sometimes advocating for policies outside the traditional healthcare domain. It's a substantial expansion of what healthcare means — and of what healthcare organizations are willing to be accountable for.
