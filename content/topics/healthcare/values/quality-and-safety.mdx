---
title: Quality and Safety
order: 5
description: Whether clinical care meets evidence-based standards, avoids preventable errors, and maintains consistent outcomes across patient populations and care settings.
---

Medical error is a leading cause of death in the United States, and much of it is preventable. The literature on quality improvement — from surgical checklists to handoff protocols to medication reconciliation — has shown repeatedly that systems and processes outperform individual vigilance when it comes to avoiding harm. Organizations that treat quality as a margin issue rather than a core mission produce systematically worse outcomes.

Quality and safety are not uniform across patient populations. Research consistently shows that clinical care is less likely to meet evidence-based standards for Black and brown patients, for women, and for people in lower-income communities — independent of clinical complexity. Quality improvement that doesn't track outcomes by race, income, gender, and primary language can improve aggregate metrics while widening disparities. What gets measured, and whose outcomes enter the denominator, determines what a quality program actually optimizes.
