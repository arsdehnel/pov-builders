---
title: Technology and Innovation
order: 8
description: The pace at which new clinical technologies, digital tools, and care delivery models are adopted and evaluated — and whether innovation serves patients or primarily serves institutions and vendors.
---

Healthcare has a complicated relationship with technology adoption. It is simultaneously too slow — many evidence-based practices take fifteen years to reach standard care — and too fast — technologies with limited evidence get adopted at scale before long-term outcomes are known. Electronic health records promised to improve care coordination and reduce error; in practice they became a major driver of clinician burnout, creating documentation burden without proportional clinical benefit.

The question for healthcare organizations isn't whether to adopt new technology but how to evaluate it honestly. Vendor claims, peer adoption pressure, and regulatory incentives all push toward adoption before evidence is clear. Building genuine evaluation capacity — running pilots with outcome measurement, tracking equity impacts, and being willing to discontinue tools that don't deliver — is more difficult than it sounds, and rarer than it should be.
