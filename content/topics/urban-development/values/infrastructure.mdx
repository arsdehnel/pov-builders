title: Infrastructure
order: 11
description: Water, sewers, power, utilities, and the systems that new development depends on — whether growth is paced to match infrastructure capacity, and who pays to upgrade it.
---

Infrastructure is the unglamorous foundation of urban development. Water and sewer capacity, electrical grid reliability, school capacity, and stormwater systems all have limits that constrain how much development a neighborhood or city can absorb without degrading service quality. When development outpaces infrastructure investment, existing residents bear the cost: older pipes fail, schools overcrowd, roads deteriorate.

Who pays for infrastructure upgrades is a live political question. Impact fees charge developers for the capacity their projects require, spreading costs to buyers and tenants through higher prices. General obligation bonds distribute costs across the tax base. State and federal grants can decouple infrastructure investment from local fiscal capacity, but are inconsistent and competitive. How a city funds the systems that growth depends on shapes both how much gets built and who can afford to live in what gets built.
