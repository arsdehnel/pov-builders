title: Housing

description: The availability, type, and density of places to live — from single-family homes to apartments, from ownership to rental, and how planning shapes who can live where.
---

Housing is the central tension in most urban planning debates. Zoning determines what kinds of housing can be built, where, and at what scale — shaping whether a city can accommodate more residents or whether scarcity drives up prices and pushes people out. Single-family-only zoning covers the majority of residential land in most American cities, limiting density even in areas with strong demand.

The question isn't just how much housing gets built but what kind and for whom. Market-rate development can increase overall supply while failing to serve low-income residents; affordable mandates can slow construction while protecting the people most at risk. Where stances diverge is on who should bear the cost of getting it right, and how much control existing residents should have over what gets built in their neighborhood.
