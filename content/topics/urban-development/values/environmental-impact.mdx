---
title: Environmental Impact
order: 7
description: Climate resilience, carbon emissions, stormwater, pollution, and the long-term sustainability of how cities grow and operate.
---

Urban development is both a source of environmental impact and a tool for reducing it. Dense, transit-oriented development reduces per-capita carbon emissions compared to sprawl; green building standards reduce operational energy use; urban tree programs and green infrastructure manage stormwater and heat. How cities grow matters enormously for their environmental footprint.

At the same time, new development often concentrates environmental harms near lower-income communities and communities of color. Industrial facilities, highways, and infrastructure sites disproportionately sit next to neighborhoods with less political power to push back. Environmental justice asks not just whether a city is becoming more sustainable in aggregate, but whose neighborhoods bear the cost of that progress and who captures the benefits.
