---
title: Affordability
order: 2
description: Whether people across income levels can afford to live in, move into, or stay in the city — and how development, displacement, and policy interact to shape who the city is for.
---

Affordability is partly about supply and partly about income. Building more market-rate housing can reduce pressure on rents over time, but it rarely produces homes that lower-income households can afford without subsidy. Inclusionary zoning requirements, community land trusts, housing vouchers, and rent stabilization are the tools cities use to close the gap — each with its own tradeoffs in terms of what gets built, by whom, and at what pace.

Displacement is the sharp edge of affordability. When rising costs push out long-term residents — particularly in lower-income and communities of color — the losses aren't just financial. Social networks, neighborhood institutions, and cultural identity get disrupted in ways that can't be measured in rent prices. How much weight those harms deserve against the benefits of growth is one of the core dividing lines in urban planning.
