---
title: Economic Activity
order: 6
description: Jobs, businesses, investment, and commercial vibrancy — whether development brings economic opportunity and who actually captures the benefits.
---

Development creates economic activity: construction jobs, commercial tenants, tax revenue, foot traffic that supports local businesses. These benefits are real, but they don't distribute evenly. Major commercial projects often source labor regionally, attract chains rather than local businesses, and generate tax increment that gets redirected into the development itself rather than city-wide services.

Community benefit agreements and local hiring requirements are attempts to ensure that economic growth translates into economic opportunity for existing residents. They work best when negotiated before approvals are granted, backed by monitoring and enforcement, and tied to measurable outcomes rather than aspirational language. The question for advocates and planners is how to structure the conditions under which development is welcome — and what to do when those conditions aren't met.
