title: Getting Around

description: Roads, public transit, cycling infrastructure, and walkability — how people move through the city and how planning choices shape what that looks like.
---

Transportation infrastructure is inseparable from land use. Cities built around car access require more roads, more parking, and more space between destinations. Cities built around transit and walking can be denser and more efficient with land, but require sustained investment in systems that the United States has historically underfunded. How a city chooses to move people determines how it can grow.

The debate over transportation is partly about efficiency and partly about values. Highway expansion reliably induces more driving rather than reducing congestion. Transit investment reduces car dependence but requires density that neighborhood opposition often blocks. Bike infrastructure improves safety and reduces emissions but can be a flashpoint in debates about who the street is for. No choice is neutral — allocating road space is allocating who can move easily and who can't.
