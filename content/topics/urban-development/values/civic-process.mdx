---
title: Civic Process
order: 12
description: Community input, public hearings, transparency, and democratic accountability in planning decisions — whether residents have a meaningful say in how their city changes.
---

Planning processes are legally required to include public participation, but required participation and meaningful participation are different things. Most public hearings favor people with time, job flexibility, and familiarity with bureaucratic processes — which skews toward existing property owners and against renters, workers, and communities with less civic infrastructure. The result is a process that claims to reflect community will while systematically underrepresenting many of the people most affected by planning decisions.

Meaningful process requires going beyond the hearing room: engagement in community spaces, in multiple languages, at accessible times, with outreach to residents who have no prior reason to trust the planning department. It also requires closing the loop — showing how input was used, explaining decisions transparently, and creating ongoing relationships rather than one-time consultations. The quality of civic process shapes whether planning decisions are legitimate and durable, or just technically legal.
