---
title: Land Use
order: 13
description: Zoning, what is permitted where, and how land is allocated between housing, commercial, industrial, and public uses — the rules that shape what gets built.
---

Zoning codes determine what can be built on every parcel of land: how tall, how dense, what mix of uses, how much parking, how far from the property line. These rules feel technical and obscure, but they are some of the most consequential policy decisions a city makes. Single-family-only zoning, exclusionary setback requirements, and mandatory parking minimums have collectively made large parts of American cities illegal to build densely, regardless of what residents or the market would choose.

Land use reform is contentious because it feels zero-sum: allowing more density in a neighborhood affects the people already there, whatever the broader benefits. The debate over zoning reform often maps onto deeper conflicts about who cities are for, whether scarcity of housing is a natural condition or a political choice, and whether existing residents should have veto power over what gets built near them.
