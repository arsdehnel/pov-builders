---
title: Historic Character
order: 3
description: The buildings, streetscapes, and cultural identity that give a neighborhood its history — what gets preserved, what gets demolished, and who decides.
---

Historic preservation protects buildings, blocks, and districts that carry architectural, cultural, or social significance. At its best, it sustains the physical record of who lived in a place and what mattered to them. At its worst, it becomes a tool for restricting new construction and locking in the preferences of whoever owned property when the designations were created — often at the expense of people who need housing but have no stake in the existing built form.

The tension sharpens in neighborhoods where historic designation overlaps with affordability pressure. Preserving a streetscape of Victorian rowhouses can protect neighborhood character, block affordable infill development, and elevate existing property values simultaneously. How much historic character is worth protecting, and whose history counts as worth preserving, is as much a political question as an architectural one.
