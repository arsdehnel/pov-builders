title: Public Safety
order: 9
description: How urban design and policy affect safety — street lighting, sight lines, traffic speed, policing, and the relationship between density, design, and crime.
---

Urban design affects safety in ways both obvious and contested. Street lighting, sight lines, active frontages, and "eyes on the street" are well-established principles for designing spaces that feel and function safely. Traffic violence — the deaths and injuries caused by car crashes — kills far more people in American cities than violent crime, and is more directly shaped by planning decisions.

The relationship between density, development, and crime is more complex than it's often presented. Dense, walkable neighborhoods with active street life tend to have lower rates of property crime than sparse, isolated areas. But new development that changes a neighborhood's income profile can shift policing patterns and community trust in ways that make existing residents feel less safe even as reported crime statistics improve. Safety, like most planning values, looks different depending on whose experience you center.
