Green space isn't evenly distributed across cities. Lower-income neighborhoods and communities of color consistently have less park access, less tree canopy, and more heat island exposure than wealthier areas — a pattern that reflects decades of disinvestment and unequal development. As climate change intensifies heat events, that gap becomes a public health gap.

New development creates both pressure and opportunity. Dense projects can displace community gardens and urban green space while also funding new parks through impact fees or dedication requirements. The question for planners and advocates is how to ensure that growth adds to the green infrastructure of the city rather than depleting it, and how to close existing gaps rather than reinforcing them.
