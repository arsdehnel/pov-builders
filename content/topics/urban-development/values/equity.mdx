---
title: Equity
order: 10
description: Whether planning decisions benefit or displace existing residents — especially lower-income communities and communities of color — and who gets to shape what the city becomes.
---

Urban planning has a long history of harming lower-income communities and communities of color: highways routed through Black neighborhoods, urban renewal projects that displaced residents without meaningful replacement housing, exclusionary zoning that preserved white suburban enclaves. Those decisions didn't happen by accident — they reflected who had power and whose interests the planning system served.

Equity in planning asks not just who benefits from growth, but who is harmed, who is included in decision-making, and whether the cumulative effect of planning decisions narrows or widens existing disparities. It requires looking past aggregate metrics — total housing units, average income growth — to whether specific communities are better or worse off, and whether they had meaningful voice in the choices that affected them.
