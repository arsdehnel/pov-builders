title: Ownership and Intellectual Property
order: 5
description: The ethical and legal questions around AI training data, copyright in AI-generated work, and how your organization's AI use reflects on your relationship with creative and content-producing communities.
---

Some critics call the mass scraping of creative works without permission the "original sin" of AI. Large language models trained on text and images that creators never consented to share and weren’t compensated for. Whether you find that framing persuasive or not, it affects how people perceive your organization's AI use, especially if you serve or work alongside creative communities.

The chapter addresses the ethical questions (should creators be compensated? is AI-generated content truly original?) and the practical ones (can you copyright AI-derived work? what's the reputational risk?) and explores how organizations can move from an extractive relationship with AI to a more reciprocal one: choosing tools with clear licensing, being transparent about AI use, and reinvesting savings into creative communities.
